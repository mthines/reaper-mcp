Worth saying once, plainly.

## The advice is a language model's, the numbers are real

The mixing advice comes from an AI model plus this project's notes on genres and workflows. It isn't an engineer sitting in your room listening to the song. The measurements, though, are real: loudness, frequency balance, stereo width and dynamics are read from your actual tracks.

So a good answer sounds like "your kick and bass both pile up around 60 Hz, here are the readings", and a suspicious one is a number it never measured. If it quotes a figure, you can ask where it came from.

## Taste is yours

How bright, how wet, how loud. When a choice is a matter of taste, it should say so and offer the other direction. If it doesn't, ask: "What would the opposite sound like?"

## It will sometimes be wrong

It can misread a track, pick a strange default, or fix the thing you liked. That's why every change is [one undo step](undo-and-safety.md) and why it measures again afterwards. Treat it like a capable assistant on day one: quick, thorough, and in need of your ears.

## What it's not good for

- Deciding what your song should be.
- Judging a performance, or whether the vocal take has feeling.
- Replacing the monitors and the room you trust.

## What it's good for

Getting the boring part done fast, so you spend your attention on the part that's yours.

> your-call: If the mix sounds worse to you, it is worse. Say so. The measurements don't outrank your ears.
