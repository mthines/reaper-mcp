A vocal can be right in the chorus and too loud in the first verse. A fader ride fixes it, but loose points drift apart as soon as you edit one copy. This does it the tidy way.

## Say this

> say: Compare my lead vocal to the mix in each section of the song and even it out with automation. Show me the numbers first.

## What happens

1. **It saves a snapshot.** Your volume envelopes are part of it, and every step is also one undo.
2. **It finds the sections** from your regions.
3. **It measures each one.** The track's loudness against the mix, section by section. Only how the gap changes matters, not the number itself.
4. **It tells you what it sees**, for example "verse 1 sits 3 LU more forward than verse 2". Parts that are far lower, like a breakdown, it asks about, because that is usually your arrangement.
5. **It draws the automation.** One automation item for each kind of section, named after it, such as "Vox Chorus". Every chorus uses the same one, so changing one chorus changes them all.

The levels sit on the volume envelope, after the plugins, so compressors and amp sims hear the same signal as before.

## After that

> say: The chorus vocal needs another 1 dB.

It edits the chorus item once and every chorus follows. A part that happens only once, like a bridge, gets its own item.

> your-call: The numbers say where the gap is. How much of it to close is taste. Ask for "half of that" if you want it to stay a little uneven.
